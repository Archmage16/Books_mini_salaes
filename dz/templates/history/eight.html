In 1885, France was in the period of the Third Republic, a time of political stability and scientific progress after years of revolution and war. One of the most important events that year was the death of Victor Hugo, the famous writer of Les Misérables and The Hunchback of Notre-Dame. His funeral became a national event, symbolizing the pride and unity of the French people. 1885 was also significant in science — Louis Pasteur successfully tested the first rabies vaccine, marking a huge advancement in medicine and public health. At the same time, France was expanding its colonial empire, especially in Africa and Asia, becoming one of the world’s major powers.
